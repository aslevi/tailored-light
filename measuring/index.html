Measuring guide

How to measure your windows.

Give us the exact opening. Don't subtract anything; we make the adjustments. Then a person on our team checks your numbers and photos, and once we've checked them your fit is guaranteed.

Open the measuring form

The form walks you through each window and sends your numbers to Dana by text. This page is the same guide, to read first or keep open while you measure.

What you need

  • A metal tape measure, the kind that snaps back. Not a sewing tape: those stretch.
  • Your phone, for photos.
  • About 10 minutes for a few windows.
  • A helper for big windows (nice, not required).
1

Inside or outside?

Pick this first. It changes what you measure.

Clean, built-in

Inside the frame

Choose it when the frame is deep enough and nothing sticks out. Leaves a thin line of light on each side.

Darkest, looks bigger

Outside, on the wall

Choose it when the frame is shallow, a crank or handle is in the way, the window isn't square, or you want it as dark as possible (bedrooms, nurseries).

How deep does the frame need to be for inside?

Under 2"Go outside. 2" to 3½"Inside works. The shade sticks out a little past the frame. 3½" or moreThe shade can sit fully inside.

These are rough. We confirm the exact depth your shade needs. Not sure? Measure both ways and we'll help you pick.

2a

Inside: 3 widths, 3 heights, 1 depth

Width, at the top, middle and bottom

Inside the frame, side to side. Write down all three.

Height, on the left, middle and right

From the top inside of the frame down to the sill. Write down all three.

Depth, at the top of the frame

From the glass to the front edge of the frame. If a crank or handle sticks out, measure from that instead.

Don't subtract anything. Send the exact numbers and we make the adjustments.

  • Measure wood to wood (or wall to wall). Not the glass, not the trim on the wall.
  • Don't round to the inch. 34 3/8 is 34 3/8.
  • Measure every window, even ones that look the same.
2b

Outside: width, height, room around it

  • Width: outside edge of the trim on the left to the outside edge on the right. No trim? Measure the opening edge to edge.
  • Height: from where you want the top of the shade (we suggest 3–4" above the trim) down to where it should stop: the top of the sill if it sticks out, or about 2" below the trim if it doesn't.
  • Room around it: we add a few inches on each side so light doesn't leak around the edges. Tell us if there's less than 3" of flat wall on either side or above, or anything in the way: crown molding, a vent, a wall corner, a curtain rod.
3

Take 3 photos per window

  • 1. The whole windowStep back so the full frame and a bit of wall show. If you can, hold the tape stretched across the width.
  • 2. A top corner, close upWith the tape showing the depth: hooked on the glass (or the crank), number readable at the front edge.
  • 3. Anything in the wayCranks, handles, locks, alarm sensors, tile, molding. Nothing there? Send the other top corner.

The photos are how we check your numbers for the fit guarantee.

4

Write it down

  • Width first, then height. Always W x H.
  • To the nearest 1/8". Between marks? Round the width down and the height up.
  • Name each window by room and number, left to right as you face it: Kitchen 1, Kitchen 2.
  • Say inside or outside.
34 3/8" x 52 1/2"

The measuring form lays this out for you. If you'd rather text it, copy and fill in:

Kitchen 1 · inside Width: top __ middle __ bottom __ Height: left __ middle __ right __ Depth: __ + 3 photos
Bedroom 1 · outside Width (trim to trim): __ Height (top of shade to bottom): __ Room on each side and above? yes / no Anything in the way? __ + 3 photos

The 6 most common mistakes

  1. Taking inches off yourself. Send the exact opening. We make the adjustments. If you subtract too, the shade comes out too small.
  2. Mixing up width and height. Width first, then height. Label them.
  3. Measuring in one spot. Windows are rarely perfectly square. Measure 3 widths and 3 heights, and measure every window, even ones that look the same.
  4. Rounding to the inch. 34 3/8 is not 34. Write it to the 1/8".
  5. Measuring the wrong thing. Inside: inside the frame, not the glass or the trim. Outside: trim edge to trim edge.
  6. Forgetting what sticks out. A crank, handle, lock or alarm sensor can stop an inside shade from fitting. Measure depth from it, and send a photo.

And use a metal tape. Cloth tapes stretch.

What happens next

We check every number.

A person on our team checks your numbers against your photos. If one looks off, we'll ask you to re-measure just that window. When everything checks out, we text you the final sizes to confirm. If our team checked your measurements and a shade doesn't fit, we remake it free.
